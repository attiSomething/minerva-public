GETTING STARTED

Your library
------------
Minerva works with one folder on your computer: your library. Inside it,
each top-level category folder (Fiction, Nonfiction, Poetry, Misc) holds one
folder per entry:

    Poetry\
      The Cold Front Came - [MB]\        <- entry folder; "- [MB]" is a project tag
        The Cold Front Came [C].txt     <- the current draft
        Archive\                        <- every older draft, never deleted
        Images\                         <- pictures that belong to the entry

  [C] marks a current, composed draft.
  [J] marks an original transcribed from a journal or scan.

Put scans, photos and documents you want to work from in the Sources folder.

Minerva keeps its own index in a hidden ".minerva" folder inside the
library. Everything else stays as ordinary folders and .txt files, so your
writing is still readable without Minerva.

Minerva never deletes anything. Saving with "Archive current draft" moves
the old version into Archive first, restoring an old draft archives the
current one, and merging moves the other entry's whole folder into this
entry's Archive.

Your first piece, start to finish
---------------------------------
Home shows a "Getting started" card with four steps: bring in a draft
(New entry, or Import files for Word, Google Docs, Markdown and others),
open it in the editor, add a submission, and make a PDF. Each step says
where that feature lives, ticks itself off when you do it anywhere in
Minerva, and can be skipped. "Hide this" puts the card away for good.

The sample library
------------------
Want to look around before pointing Minerva at your own writing? "Explore
the sample library" (on the setup screen, the Getting started card, or
Settings > Library) opens a small library of made-up poems, stories, a
project and some submissions. It lives in its own folder beside Minerva's
settings, never inside your library, so you can try anything. A strip on
Home says when the sample is open, with "Back to my library" and "Start
the sample over" (which puts it back the way it began).

Synced folders (Obsidian and other notes)
-----------------------------------------
If you keep writing in Obsidian, or any folder of Markdown or text notes,
add it under Settings > Synced folders. Minerva only reads that folder;
it never changes, moves or deletes anything in it.

Each time you open Home, Minerva looks for notes that are new or have
changed since last time and lists them in a strip; nothing happens until
you press Review. There you tick what to bring in: a new note becomes a
new entry, and a changed note becomes a new version of its entry, with
the old draft kept in Archive. If you've also edited that entry in
Minerva since the last sync, it's marked and left unticked, so your edits
are never replaced without you choosing it. "Don't offer this again"
skips a note for good.

"Folders and tags..." sets where notes go: each top folder in the notes
folder picks a category (or "Don't bring in", for daily notes and the
like), and anything else goes to the category you chose. Tags in a
note's front matter or written as #tag become Minerva tags; rename one or
leave it out on the same screen. Hidden folders such as .obsidian and
.trash are skipped.

AI features (optional)
----------------------
AI-assisted scanning and AI-assisted organizing are off unless you turn them
on in Settings. You bring your own provider and key: Anthropic (Claude),
OpenAI, Google Gemini, or a local model through Ollama. Keys are stored in
Windows Credential Manager on this computer, not in your library.

For the free, private route, press "Set up free local AI" (Settings > Free
and private AI, the AI steps of setup, or the panel that appears when an
AI feature is off). Minerva checks this computer's memory, graphics card
and disk space, suggests a model sized to it with its download size and
speed, then installs Ollama if needed, downloads the model, reads a test
page with it and turns the features on.

Typed and printed scans don't need AI at all: on Windows, Scans reads each
page with Windows' own text recognition first, free and offline, and only
sends handwritten pages to the AI model (or leaves them for later when AI
is off). Settings > AI-assisted scanning can turn this off.

Two how-tos on the Help tab cover cost and privacy: "Running AI on Your
Computer" (free and private, with Ollama) and "Using an AI Service" (what
Anthropic, OpenAI and Google charge and do with your writing). Every cost
estimate in Minerva links to both, and so does the panel that appears if
you use an AI feature while it's off.

Coming up
---------
Home's "Coming up" panel lists the next 30 days: deadlines for planned
submissions, venues whose reading period opens or closes (from the
reading periods saved in Venues, such as "Sep to Nov, Feb to Apr"),
submissions past the date a reply was expected, and pieces with a final
draft that aren't out anywhere. It only appears when there's something
in it.

Goals
-----
On Home, "Set goals" takes a daily and/or weekly word goal. Progress counts
words added across the whole library since the day (or week, from Monday)
began, so cutting words counts against it. A project can have a target
length too: set it on the project's page, and it shows on Home beside your
other goals.

Library check
-------------
Each time Minerva opens or rescans the library, it looks for things it
can't make sense of: an entry folder with no draft (it won't show up in
Entries), several drafts tagged [C], drafts that aren't saved as UTF-8 or
can't be opened, empty drafts, and files sitting loose in a category
folder. Home shows how many there are; "Review" lists them, with buttons to
open the entry or show the file in its folder. Minerva only reports these;
it never changes them. Settings > Library > "Check library" opens the same
list.

Switching libraries
-------------------
Settings > Library > "Open a different library…". Minerva remembers the
last library you opened and goes straight back to it next time.

Your name and style
-------------------
Settings > Customization. Your name is only used to greet you on Home;
Minerva has no accounts. Pick a style to change how every page looks:
Athena (the original blues and gold), Dark Athena, Tarofied, Fifth Impact
or Liched Up. The how-to pictures follow the style too.

Backups
-------
Settings > Backups. Choose a folder off this drive (OneDrive, an external
drive, a USB stick), then press "Back up now". Each backup is one dated zip
of your whole library, Minerva's index included; old backups are never
overwritten or deleted, so remove ones you no longer want yourself.

"Changes only" saves just the files changed since the last backup. It's
quicker, but to restore you need the last full backup as well.

Home reminds you when a backup is due; set how often (or never) under
"Remind me on Home". To restore, unzip a backup somewhere and open that
folder with "Open a different library…".

Export everything
-----------------
Settings > Export everything makes a copy of all your work that opens
without Minerva: every file in your library in its own folders, plus
"Minerva records", which spell out what Minerva knows about your writing.
Notes come out as Markdown; entries, tags, collections, projects, outlines,
submissions, venues and people as spreadsheets (CSV, for Excel, Google
Sheets or Numbers); goals and settings as plain text, with no keys or
passwords. A README in the export says what each file is.

Choose a folder outside your library and whether you want one .zip file or
a folder. Scans, pictures and recordings are left out unless you tick
"Include scans, pictures and recordings". Your library isn't changed.

An export is for reading your work anywhere else, or keeping a copy in a
form that will outlast any app. To move your library to a new computer or
restore it into Minerva, use a backup instead.
