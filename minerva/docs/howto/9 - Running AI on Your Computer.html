Running AI on Your Computer

Minerva's AI features can use a model that runs on your own computer, through a free program called Ollama. Nothing you write is sent anywhere, there's no account or key, and it costs nothing per use. The trade-offs are speed, a large download, and accuracy that's a step behind the paid services, especially on handwriting.

The easy way: let Minerva set it up

Press Set up free local AI: it's in Settings under Free and private AI, on the AI steps of first-time setup, and on the panel that appears when you try an AI feature that isn't set up. Minerva looks at this computer's memory, graphics card and free disk space, suggests a model sized to it, and tells you the download size and roughly how fast it will be before anything starts. Then, when you say go, it:

  1. installs Ollama, if it isn't already (on Windows; Windows may ask you to allow the installer),
  2. downloads the model,
  3. reads a typed test page with it, so you can see it work and how long a page takes,
  4. and turns on the AI features you ticked, using that model.

You can close the window and keep writing while it downloads. If it's stopped partway, starting again picks up where it left off. If the test page comes back garbled, nothing is turned on until you say so. The rest of this guide is the same thing done by hand, for anyone who wants a different model.

1. Check your computer

A local model needs memory more than anything. With 16 GB of RAM (or a graphics card with 8 GB of its own), an 8 billion-parameter model runs comfortably. With 8 GB of RAM, use the smaller sizes noted below and expect it to be slow. A page that takes a paid service a few seconds can take a minute or more on a laptop without a graphics card. Plan on 5 to 10 GB of disk space per model.

2. Install Ollama

Download Ollama for Windows from ollama.com and install it. It runs quietly in the background and starts with Windows. Minerva talks to it at localhost, on this computer only.

3. Pull a model

Open a Command Prompt and type ollama pull followed by a model name. Good starting points as of October 2026:

  • For scanning (reading pages), a vision model: qwen3-vl:8b (a 6 GB download), or qwen3-vl:4b (3 GB) on a smaller computer.
  • For organizing and proofreading, a text model: gemma3:12b (8 GB), or gemma3:4b (3 GB). The vision models above can do this job too, if you'd rather download one model.

New models come out often; the list at ollama.com/library shows which can read images.

4. Point Minerva at it

In Settings, under AI-assisted scanning or organizing, turn the feature on, choose Local model (Ollama), and press Test connection. The models you've pulled appear in the Model list; pick one and press Save.

5. Know what to expect

Local models read typed and neatly printed pages well. Joined-up handwriting is still unreliable on local models, by most accounts, so plan on more proofreading, or use a paid service for a notebook you care about and keep the local model for organizing and proofreading. Cost estimates in Minerva show tokens but no price for a local model, because there isn't one.

Good to know: you can mix: a local model for organizing (which sees all your drafts) and a paid service just for scanning, or the other way round. Each feature has its own setting.
