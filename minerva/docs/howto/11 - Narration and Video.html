Narration and Video

Hearing a poem read back is one of the best ways to catch what the eye skips, and a recording is something to share. Minerva can read an entry, or a whole chapbook, into an MP3: free with the voices already on your computer, or with ElevenLabs if you want a more natural voice and have an account.

1. Make a narration

Open an entry and press Narrate in its Share panel, or open a project and press Audio in its Publish panel. Choose a voice and a pace, whether each title is read, and press Make audio. You can close the window and keep writing while it works. The file goes into the Exports folder like any other export, and a project uses each entry's final draft, as the proof does.

2. One file or one per piece

A project can become one file with a chapter for each piece, which audiobook and podcast players show as a list you can skip through, or a separate file for each piece. A pause separates one piece from the next.

3. The free audio and video tool

Minerva puts the recording together itself, as a WAV file. To make the smaller MP3 that phones, players and websites expect, it uses FFmpeg, a free tool used by most video and audio programs. The first time you choose MP3, Minerva offers to download it once (about 31 MB). It's kept in Minerva's own settings folder and nothing is installed elsewhere. You can always choose WAV instead.

4. This computer's voices

These are the voices Read aloud uses, built into Windows. They cost nothing and the writing never leaves your computer. Windows has more voices for more languages in Settings > Time & language > Speech; after adding one, it appears in Minerva's list.

5. ElevenLabs: cost and privacy

ElevenLabs makes voices that sound much closer to a person reading. You need an account at elevenlabs.io and an API key from its developer settings; paste the key in the Narrate window and Minerva keeps it in Windows Credential Manager, like an AI key.

  • Cost. ElevenLabs plans come with a monthly allowance of credits, and the voice Minerva uses takes one credit per character, spaces included. A one-page poem is a few hundred credits; a 40-page chapbook is roughly 40,000 to 60,000. Before anything is sent, Minerva shows how many credits a narration will use and how many your account has left this month.
  • Privacy. The text is sent to ElevenLabs to be read, and the audio is kept in your account's history there. Read ElevenLabs' privacy policy before narrating unpublished work, or use this computer's voices, which send nothing anywhere.
  • Using the audio. ElevenLabs' plans differ on whether you can sell or publish what you make, and the free plan asks for credit to ElevenLabs. Check your plan's terms before posting or selling a recording.

6. Make a video

A narration can become a video that shows the words a stanza at a time, with the line being read in bright white. Press Video next to Narrate or Audio, or Make a video from it when a narration finishes. Choose a shape (square for Instagram and Facebook posts, vertical for Reels, TikTok and Shorts, widescreen for YouTube) and a background: the project's front cover, a picture of your own, or plain navy. Pictures are darkened and softened so the words stay readable, and the preview shows a real screen before you start.

The video is made on this computer, with the same free audio and video tool, and saved as an MP4 in Exports. Nothing is uploaded anywhere.

Good to know: the video follows the timing Minerva noted when the narration was made (kept in the hidden .minerva folder), so make the narration in Minerva first. Editing the poem afterwards doesn't change an existing narration or its video; narrate it again to pick up the changes.
