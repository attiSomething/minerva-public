Tracking Where a Poem Is Out

The Submissions tab is a log of every piece you've sent out: where, when, the fee, and what came back. It reminds you what's overdue and, when something is accepted, helps you tidy up the rest.

1. Record a submission

Open the entry and press Add in its Submissions panel, or use Add submission on the Submissions tab. Fill in where it went, the date, the fee and, if you know it, how long they usually take to reply. A submission can be Planned before you send it.

2. Bring in the log you already keep

If you've been tracking in a spreadsheet, save it as CSV and use Import... on the Submissions tab. Minerva guesses which column is which, matches each row to an entry by title, and shows every row before saving: ready, not found, unclear, or already recorded. Nothing is skipped without a reason.

3. From Submittable or The Grinder

Neither service lets other apps sign in on your behalf, but both let you download your history as a CSV file, and Minerva reads their files as they come:

  • Submittable: sign in, open the Submissions tab, and press the download button (a folder with an arrow).
  • The Grinder: sign in, go to my account then manage submissions, tick every box under by status, press show submissions, then generate export.

Choose the file with Import...; Minerva recognizes where it's from, and you can change that at the top of the preview. Their status words are understood ("In-Progress" is waiting, "Form Rejection" is declined), and the service's own word is kept in the notes. Download a fresh export now and then and import it again: submissions already in Minerva get their new status and response date instead of being added twice.

From your email. To keep up without exporting again, bring in the emails instead: the "we received your submission" confirmations and the decisions that come through Submittable (and Moksha, Duosuma and Chill Subs). Put them in their own label or folder, export that as an .mbox file (Gmail: Google Takeout, choosing just that label; Apple Mail: Mailbox > Export Mailbox; Thunderbird: the ImportExportTools add-on) or save the messages as .eml files, and choose the file or files in Import.... Minerva finds the venue, the piece (by its title) and what happened, combines a confirmation with its later decision, and shows the same preview: new records, updates to ones you already have, and emails whose piece it couldn't tell, which you can match by hand. Minerva doesn't sign in to your email.

Duotrope's terms of use say data downloaded from it is for your personal use and can't be used to create another database, so Minerva doesn't offer a Duotrope import.

Cover letters and bios. Snippets on the Submissions tab keeps the text you paste again and again: a cover letter, a short and a long bio, acknowledgments. Write {venue} or {title} where the magazine's name or the piece's title goes, and {name} or {email} for your details from Settings > Manuscript details. In a submission, pick a snippet and press Copy to paste it, filled in, into the magazine's form, or Add to notes to keep a copy of what you sent.

4. Check what's waiting

Tick Still waiting to see only open submissions. Ones past their expected reply date are marked overdue, and Home counts them. Filters for status, venue, category, project and dates narrow the list, and the tiles add up fees and your acceptance rate for what's shown.

5. When a reply comes

Change the status. An acceptance offers to mark the entry Published (with where) and to withdraw it from any other place it's still waiting, so simultaneous submissions don't get forgotten.

6. Keep notes on venues

The Venues view keeps reading periods, fees, typical response time, line limits and your own notes for each magazine or press, with the history of everything you've sent there. When a piece is longer than a venue's line limit, the submission form says so.

Coming up on Home gathers what needs you in the next 30 days: deadlines for submissions you've planned, venues whose reading period opens or closes (written the way magazines write them, like "Sep to Nov, Feb to Apr"), submissions past the date a reply was expected, and pieces with a final draft that aren't out anywhere. Click any of them to open it.

7. Find places to send it

In a piece's or project's edit view, Find places to send it under Submissions starts with Your venues: the places you already keep notes on, best fit first. A venue whose line limit the piece is over drops to the bottom, a place that has taken your work before rises, and places you've already sent it to are listed apart. This part is free and never leaves your computer.

Suggest places asks the AI you chose in Settings (under organizing and editing) for magazines, presses and contests that publish this kind of work. Open What the AI is told to see exactly what is sent. It's the kind of writing and its length, and the tags. You can add a line about what it's about, and the title if you tick it. The writing itself goes only if you tick Let the AI read the writing itself.

What the AI says about a venue is from its memory and can be out of date, so each suggestion is marked "not checked". Check their website reads the venue's own home page and its guidelines page. It shows the reading period, fee, response time, line limit, payment and how to submit, each with the sentence it came from and a link to the page. A detail goes in only when that sentence is really on their page. Add to Venues saves the venue, with what was checked and where it came from in its notes.

Good to know: Minerva doesn't read submission directories such as Duotrope, Submittable or Chill Subs, whose terms don't allow automated reading. Use them in your browser, and add what you find with Add venue. Always read a venue's guidelines yourself before you send.
Good to know: submissions live in Minerva's index, not in your text files, and follow an entry through renames and merges.
