Using an AI Service

An AI service is the quickest way to good results, and the best at handwriting. You pay the provider directly for what you use, usually pennies, and your writing goes to their servers to be read. Here is what that costs and what they do with it, checked against each provider's own pages in October 2026.

1. Pick a provider and get a key

Minerva works with Anthropic (Claude), OpenAI and Google Gemini. Make an account on the provider's developer site (console.anthropic.com, platform.openai.com or aistudio.google.com), add a payment method, and create an API key. Paste it in Settings; Minerva keeps it in Windows Credential Manager, never in your library or anywhere else.

2. What it costs

Services charge per "token", roughly three-quarters of a word, with separate prices for what goes in and what comes back. Some rough figures at October 2026 list prices:

  • Reading one notebook page: 2 to 3 cents with Claude Sonnet 5.5, about 5 cents with Claude Opus 5.5, and about a tenth of a cent with Claude Haiku 5.5. A 200-page notebook comes to about $5 on Sonnet.
  • Proofreading a one-page poem: a cent or two on Sonnet or a similar model.
  • OpenAI and Google price their models in the same range, from a fraction of a cent to a few cents per page, depending on the model.

Before any job that sends more than a page or two, Minerva shows an estimate and waits for you to say yes. Pages already read are kept, so stopping and restarting never pays twice.

3. Set a spending limit

Each provider's billing page lets you prepay a set amount or set a monthly limit, and email you as you approach it. Setting a limit of a few dollars before your first big scan is a good habit.

4. What happens to your writing

All three say that writing sent through their paid API is not used to train their models unless you opt in. Anthropic and OpenAI keep it for up to 30 days to check for abuse, then delete it. Google's free tier is different: its pricing page says content sent on the free tier is used to improve Google's products, so Minerva doesn't suggest free tiers for unpublished work.

5. Choosing a model

For handwriting, a provider's most capable model is worth the extra cents; for printed pages and for organizing, the mid-range model is plenty. You can set scanning and organizing separately, and change either at any time in Settings.

Good to know: prices and policies change. Your provider's own pricing and privacy pages are the final word; if you'd rather nothing leave your computer at all, see Running AI on Your Computer.
